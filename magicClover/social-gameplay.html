3. Self-Assessment

If you think your or someone else's computer gameplay is becoming problematic, consider the following questions:

  • Do you often re-live gameplay experiences or think about future ones?
  • Do you hide or lie about your gameplay?
  • Do you get angry when someone or something interrupts a game?
  • Have you taken a break from gameplay and binged uncontrollably upon returning?
  • When upset, do you soothe yourself with games or plans to game?
  • Do you play casino or poker games in the early morning?
  • Do you find ways to game when away from home?
  • Do you set limits and then break them, playing longer than intended?
  • Do you lose hours of sleep to gameplay?
  • Have you called in sick or late to work, or skipped classes, to play a game?
  • Have you sworn off a game, uninstalled it, and later returned to it?
  • Do you feel guilt or shame around your gameplay?
  • Does your gameplay contribute to arguments in your relationships?
  • Has gameplay taken the place of hobbies or sports you used to enjoy?
  • Do you forget appointments, responsibilities, or deadlines when playing online?
  • Do you become irritated or defensive when people suggest you may be playing online too much?
  • Have your hours spent playing online increased over time?
  • Do you skip social events to game?
  • Have you lost contact with friends or family since playing online?
  • Do you have intense highs, lows, anger, or fear while playing online?

The more questions you answer "yes" to, the more likely it is that you are having difficulty with gameplay. To speak with someone who can provide advice and support, contact the Support Organization in Section 4.
